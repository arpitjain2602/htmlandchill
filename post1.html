Finding Signal in Product Noise

By Arpit Jain • 4 min read • Product Strategy

In growth and product management, lack of data is almost never the issue anymore. In fact, most teams suffer from the exact opposite condition: hyper-instrumented noise.

When dashboards are overloaded with dozens of conversion funnels, heatmaps, and event triggers, teams can easily confuse movement with progress. A cohort conversion rate bumps 1.2% in one segment while dipping 0.8% in another, and suddenly three sprints are lost trying to dissect a phantom fluctuation.

"Measurement without a mechanistic hypothesis isn't product science—it's just expensive astrology with charts."

1. The Trap of Proxy Metrics

A common trap is optimizing for proxies that drift away from true customer utility. In marketplace and dynamic pricing systems, for instance, high session engagement or frequency of parameter tweaks might look great on an activity dashboard. But if customers are tweaking parameters constantly, it often means they don't trust the automated pricing algorithm—not that they love using the tool.

Signal is found at the intersection of behavioral commitment and economic outcome:

  • Noise: Page views, feature click counts, and session duration.
  • Signal: User willingness to accept algorithmic suggestions, recurring revenue retention, and time-to-value completion.

2. Hypotheses Before Dashboards

Before launching any A/B experiment or feature branch, we force a simple discipline: write down the causal mechanism in plain English.

If we change the way listing price elasticity is displayed on the calendar view, why will a customer change their behavior? What psychological barrier or friction is being eliminated? If the answer relies on "users will explore the data more," it's almost certainly noise. If the answer is "users currently lack conviction that neighboring listings are booked at this rate, so surfacing neighborhood comps removes pricing hesitation," you have a testable signal.

3. Protecting Momentum

Finding signal requires saying no to the temptation to explain every outlier. When you ruthlessly narrow your focus to the single causal metric that drives durable value, product teams move faster, build with conviction, and ship software that actually moves the needle.
