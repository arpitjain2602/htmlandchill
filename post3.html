The Art of Scrappy 0-to-1 PM Execution

By Arpit Jain • 4 min read • 0-to-1 & Product Velocity

In established organizations, there is a natural gravitational pull toward process: alignment reviews, 40-page PRDs, risk assessments, and multi-team roadmapping syncs.

While governance makes sense at scale, it is toxic to early-stage 0-to-1 product development. When launching something that doesn't yet exist—whether it's Revenue Estimator Pro inside PriceLabs or an independent side project like Offers Hub—velocity and tactile feedback are everything.

"A rough, clickable prototype in front of three target customers teaches you more in 45 minutes than three weeks of internal debate."

1. Prototypes Over Slide Decks

Slide decks are dangerous because they make unvalidated assumptions look clean and settled. Slides hide edge cases, interaction friction, and cognitive load behind bullet points.

When taking an idea from 0 to 1, build the thinnest possible vertical slice. Even if the backend is a Google Sheet or hardcoded JSON, get a real interactive artifact into hands immediately. The questions that emerge from touching real software are fundamentally different from abstract feedback on slides.

2. Shrink the Feedback Loop to Hours

The primary competitive moat of small teams and agile builders is cycle time:

  • Hear user feedback on Monday morning.
  • Write the prototype slice on Monday afternoon.
  • Deploy a live test branch by Tuesday.

When cycle time is measured in hours rather than quarters, you can afford to be wrong repeatedly because the cost of testing is near zero.

3. Knowing When to Shift Gears

Scrappy 0-to-1 execution doesn't mean abandoning rigor—it means prioritizing discovery until product-market fit signal is unmistakable. Once the core engine works ($1M+ ARR or retention curve flattening), you intentionally switch gears into scalability, data pipelines, and system reliability.
